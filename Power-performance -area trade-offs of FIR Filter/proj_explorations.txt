Here is the final, locked-in lineup of your 6 Verilog architectures:

1. The Naive Baseline (Direct Form)
Concept: The tutorial code.

Metrics: Terrible $F_{max}$, high power, high area.

2. The Pipelined Baseline (Speed Focus)
Concept: Adding flip-flops between the multiplier and adder stages of the Baseline.

Metrics: Massive $F_{max}$ boost, lower dynamic power (due to reduced glitching), but higher FF area.

3. The Symmetric Architecture (DSP Math Focus)
Concept: Adding mirrored taps before multiplying (the classic DSP trick).

Metrics: DSP slices cut in half, massive area and static power savings.

4. The Fully Serial MAC (Extreme Area Focus)
Concept: A single multiplier and adder looped 8 times with a state machine (Time-Multiplexed).

Metrics: Almost zero area, but terrible latency and throughput (takes 8 clocks per sample).

5. The Multiplier-less CSD (Extreme Power Focus)
Concept: Replacing all DSP multipliers with hardwired bit-shifts (Canonic Signed Digit).

Metrics: Zero DSP slices used. The absolute lowest dynamic power possible while maintaining 1-cycle throughput.

6. The "Hybrid" (The Thesis Conclusion)
Concept: You combine Symmetry + CSD Bit-Shifting + Pipelining.

Metrics: This will be the ultimate winner. It uses 0 DSP slices (CSD), cuts the adder tree in half (Symmetry), and hits a massive clock speed (Pipelining).
